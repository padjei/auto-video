# NISTA Technologies — Launch Film
## 60-second narration script (v1)

**Total spoken words: 138** · Target pace 2.3 w/s · Estimated runtime 60.0s
**CTA:** "Modernize with confidence." (final line, Beat 8)
**Platform note:** LinkedIn sound-off first. Every ON-SCREEN line is ≤6 words and must be
legible at feed size. Narration is the second layer, not the primary carrier.

## Beat 1 — 0:00–0:06 · HOOK

**NARRATION:**
Some software is allowed to fail quietly. The systems you run are not.

**ON-SCREEN:**
`Your systems cannot fail quietly.`

**INTENT:**
Stop the scroll in 3 seconds with sound off. Frame the stakes from the viewer's side of the
desk, not the vendor's. No logo, no name yet — earn the next five seconds first.

## Beat 2 — 0:06–0:15 · PROBLEM

**NARRATION:**
Modernization rarely fails at the whiteboard. It fails in the distance between those who
plan and those who build.

**ON-SCREEN:**
`Modernization fails in delivery.`

**INTENT:**
Name the failure mode a federal CIO has actually lived through. This is the diagnosis that
makes the engineering-led differentiator in Beat 4 land as a solution rather than a slogan.

## Guardrail compliance notes

- **No authorization claims.** Nothing in this script references FedRAMP, ATO, IL levels, or
  DoD authorization. Beat 6 was written around *posture* (zero-trust, hardened defaults,
  secure SDLC) precisely because authorization language is unavailable.
- **No past performance.** Beats 4 and 5 originally wanted a customer-outcome proof point.
  Since no agency customers, awards, or contract vehicles may be cited, both beats were
  rebuilt around engineering structure — *who does the work* and *when requirements enter the
  lifecycle*. This is the substitution the brief anticipated.
- **No SOC 2 / NIST claims.** Deliberately omitted entirely rather than risk "readiness"
  language being misread on a sound-off overlay as achieved certification.
- **No invented metrics.** No uptime figures, headcount, customer counts, or percentages
  appear in narration or on screen.
- **RhemaPresenter excluded** per brief.
- **"Certified"** appears only inside the on-screen phrase "Certified Small Business," where
  its meaning is unambiguous. Narration says "registered, active, and contract-ready" instead
  of "certified" so the word cannot be misheard as a security certification.
- **"Contract-ready"** is supported by active SAM.gov registration, UEI, CAGE, and Small
  Business certification. It asserts the ability to be contracted with, not any award.

### Over-stuffed beats

**Beat 2 (2.44 w/s) — the tightest beat in the film.** "Modernization rarely fails at the
whiteboard" is a 7-word sentence that only works if the read slows down on "whiteboard." At
2.44 w/s the VO will have to clip it. Two options:

1. *Preferred:* trim to **20 words** — "Modernization rarely fails at the whiteboard. It
   fails in the distance between those who plan and those who build." Drops "the work" and
   one "it." Brings the beat to 2.22 w/s and total script to 136 words (still in range).
2. Extend Beat 2 to 0:06–0:16 and pull one second from Beat 4 (which has the most headroom
   in its second sentence).

**Beat 4 (2.40 w/s) — dense for its importance, not for its length.** Ten seconds is enough
time, but this is the beat that has to change the viewer's mind, and the second sentence
("The engineer who scopes it is the engineer who ships it") needs a distinct pause before it
to read as a conclusion rather than a continuation. Direct the VO to place a 0.5s beat after
"intermediaries" and cut the visual there. If that pause cannot be found in the mix, drop
"designed," from the first sentence — "architected and delivered by engineers" carries the
same meaning at 23 words.

**No beat is under-stuffed.** Beats 1 and 8 are intentionally sparse: the hook needs air to
work sound-off, and the CTA needs the last word to sit alone.

### Structural note for the edit

Peak information density runs 0:06–0:33 (Beats 2–4), which is correct — that is the window
where a skeptical federal buyer decides whether to keep watching. The back third
(0:42–1:00) deliberately drops to a lower word rate so the credential card and the CTA get
visual dwell time rather than competing with narration.
